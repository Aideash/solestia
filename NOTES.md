Because we use apparent solar time rather than a uniform count, Mercury's hand genuinely creeps backwards for a few days around perihelion — its Sun reverses in the sky. I left that in as real behavior; switching to a mean Sun is a one-line change in localSolarTime if it reads as a glitch to you.

The clocks and P_rot share one longitude system, chosen at the top of the page (default IAU). IAU is WGCCRE 2015 / pck00011: System III for Jupiter, Saturn and Uranus, and Karkoschka's ~15.97 h south-polar cloud period for Neptune (the cartographic W since 2015). Magnetic is that same System III where IAU already is, and Voyager radio (~16.11 h, Seidelmann et al. 2002) for Neptune. Cloud is Jupiter System I, Saturn's equatorial System I (10h 14m; IAU no longer tabulates it), and Neptune's IAU 2015 W; Uranus and the inner planets have no separate cloud or magnetic W, so they stay on IAU. Jupiter System II is stored but not selectable.
